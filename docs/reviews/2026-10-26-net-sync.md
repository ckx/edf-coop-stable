# Network sync review, 2026-10-26

Review date: 2026-10-26.

**Scope:** the repository at `46c4e1bf0f8c4637ac0ed8736d45e3aec348f336` (`main`, clean). All production C++ in
`src/` (EOS hooks, direct link, reliable layer, wire format, fragments, hold, lobby marker, config, UPnP, netif) and
`game/` (player sync, hit authority, netcode gate, spectator camera, peer timeout, weapon guard, laser trace, plugin
entry), `docs/net-re/` and `docs/spectate/`, the test list and the GameNet README. Priority areas, as asked: the
spectator camera in its locked mode (the game's follow camera), and how another player's projectiles end up where
that player saw them land.

**Excluded:** no build and no test run. No live play. `dist/`, `tools/`, `third_party/`, the build and release
scripts, `game/log.cpp`, `game/crashlog.cpp`, `game/radar.cpp` and `game/shieldtrace.cpp` were skimmed, not reviewed.
Test bodies were not read beyond their names and the GameNet README. The all-forces notes that `damage.md` cites
(`online-re.md`, `bullet-pass-re.md`) are not in this workspace, so what they say about NetworkBullet is taken from
`damage.md`'s summary.

**Method:** one reviewer read every production file in the scope, then the research notes, then disassembled
`LaserBullet01`'s NetworkBullet interface (`tools/pe.py` against the supported `EDF.dll`, TimeDateStamp
`0x678CCB46`) to find out what a remote bullet copy receives from its owner, which no note in the repository records.
Line numbers refer to the reviewed revision.

`[C]` means the path is established from source or from the disassembly named. `[A]` means a material assumption
remains (game behaviour that only a live capture settles); the assumption is named. Neither marker claims a
reproduction.

## Summary

The transport and the hit authority hold up. Nothing found in `src/` loses, duplicates or misroutes game data; the
hold, the owner pin, the reliable layer, the fragments and the link crypto match their comments and the earlier
review's fixes are in. The game part verifies every byte it patches and refuses cleanly.

The two areas the owner asked about are limited by the same thing, which the plugin does not touch yet: how a
remote player's **aim** and **record cadence** reach other machines.

- A remote copy's aim (`+0x1230/+0x1234`) is written only when a record with mask bit 2 arrives. On foot that is
  every sixth sync frame; in a vehicle or while down it never is. The locked spectator camera reads that field
  through the ghost camera's target matrix, so it turns in 100 ms steps and freezes through a ride. The plugin logs
  the freeze (3.3.0) but does nothing about it (finding 1).
- Player sync writes a block every second sync frame, but receivers accept one block in three, 100 ms apart
  (`docs/net-re/player.md`, 2026-10-22). Two thirds of the block bytes never arrive, the extrapolation runs over
  100 ms instead of 33 ms, and nobody has established where the thinning happens. One INI experiment tells
  (finding 2).
- A remote bullet copy is not left to itself: the owner sends it per-bullet messages through its NetworkBullet
  interface (two vectors, hit and attach events with an object reference). The laser diagnostic hooks exactly
  that receive, but snapshots the copy before the message is applied and never decodes it, so the logs cannot say
  whether a copy follows the owner's trajectory (finding 3). Decoding it is the capture `laser.md` says is missing.

Everything else is Minor: a dead lock pair, a stale comment, a four-entry track table that thrashes at mission end,
two INI ranges that clamp without a warning, a statistic that overcounts, and a diagnostic that is still on by
default in a stable release.

None found.

## Major

### 1. [C] Remote aim arrives in 100 ms steps, never in a vehicle, and nothing smooths it

**Location:** `game/spectate.cpp:534-547` (`NoteStaleAim`, logs the freeze and nothing else), `:595-598` (the locked
camera clears the orbit byte and shows the game's view); `game/netplayer.h:34-39` (the player block carries
position and velocity, no aim); `docs/spectate/research.md`, "Free and locked camera, 2026-10-24" (the evidence:
mask builder `0x59FB10` includes aim every sixth sync frame on foot, never with a vehicle reference or while down;
the receiver `0x592180` stores each record as it arrives; the 2026-10-17 capture shows the camera forward equal to
the received angles in every sample).

The locked camera is the game's follow camera and shows the teammate's facing as received. The plugin's player
sync improved position and velocity at 30 Hz written, 10 Hz delivered, and left aim alone, so a watched teammate's
view turns in up to 100 ms steps, stalls through a lost record, and during a ride points along the boarding heading
while the vehicle turns. The same stale aim is what every remote copy's pose and weapon direction show on other
machines; whether a remote bullet's direction depends on it is finding 3's question.

**Failure scenario:** a downed player presses E for the locked camera while the watched teammate drives a tank
across the map. The view stays fixed along the heading the teammate had when boarding and the tank drives out of
it. On foot, every turn the teammate makes shows as a sequence of jumps, one per received record.

**Fix shape:** two parts, the first local and cheap, the second a wire change.

- Locked camera without a wire change: keep the orbit mechanism on in both modes (byte `+0x40`, angles `+0x50`,
  proven for the free camera since 0.4.0) and in locked mode feed it the watched soldier's received aim,
  interpolated per frame between the last two received samples by their arrival times (a one-interval delay, no
  overshoot), and clamp extrapolation to one interval. While the soldier holds a vehicle reference (`+0x1548`,
  control use count), the game sends no aim: fall back to the yaw of the soldier's own matrix row `+0x80` (what
  boarding sets, `0x5705A5`) with pitch 0, so the view turns with the vehicle. This changes the scope decision
  "locked = the game's follow camera as it is" and needs the owner's yes; the view is the same offsets and
  smoothing, only its rotation source changes. Sends nothing; no gate.
- Aim in the player block: version 2 of the block adds pitch, yaw and their rates (4 floats, 47 bytes), behind
  `kNetProtocol + 1` so mixed rooms fall back. The slot 55 hook, which already drives the copy's position,
  writes `+0x1230/+0x1234` for a remote copy with a fresh sample each frame (sample plus rate times age, capped as
  position is). The game's own bit 2 write keeps happening; the plugin's write after it wins for that frame. Worth
  doing only once finding 3 has shown that the copy's aim matters for something besides the camera; for the camera
  alone the first part is enough.

### 2. [C] Player blocks are written at 30 Hz and delivered at 10 Hz, cause not established

**Location:** `game/netplayer_game.cpp:332` (`SendIntervalFrames` default 2), `:199-203` (`Sending`: the block goes
into every second sync frame's record), `game/netplayer.h:64-72` (the velocity window widened to 90 ms to cope with
the delivered cadence); `docs/net-re/player.md`, "2026-10-22 estimator windup and delivery cadence" ("every
accepted block is 3 sequence numbers and 100 ms after the last ... where the game thins them is not established").

Sync frames count at 60 Hz (`+0x1820`). With the block on even frames and every third block arriving, the ones
that arrive share one residue modulo 6. The game's own mask puts position on frames 3 mod 6 and aim on frames
0 mod 6 and nothing heavier on the other four. The simplest reading is that only records carrying one of those two
bits leave the machine, or survive the 450-byte per-frame budget, and the block rides out only on the 0 mod 6
record. That is a hypothesis; the alternative, one record per object per 100 ms somewhere in the packet
controller, fits the same numbers.

**Failure scenario:** every remote copy is driven from a sample up to 100 ms old plus one-way delay, and a dash
started between two delivered samples is extrapolated from a velocity measured 90 ms earlier. The 2026-10-22 bursts
show the 5 to 9 m errors that follow. Two thirds of the block bytes the sender writes are discarded.

**Fix shape:** measure before changing code. One session with `[NetPlayer] SendIntervalFrames=3` (blocks on
frames 0 and 3 mod 6, both of which carry a game bit) and the existing `PLAYER sync` counters on both sides: if
`received` per 30 s doubles, the thinning is per record and the fix is to put the block only on frames the game
sends anyway (interval 3, or "frames with bit 1 or bit 2 set", read from the mask the hook already has) and to
measure the velocity over 50 ms. If it stays at 10 Hz, the thinning is downstream and the next step is the packet
controller's record assembly (`0x75D2E0`, the 450-byte budget, and the datagram flush at `0x12CEA10`). Either way,
record the answer in `player.md`, which currently tunes around the symptom.

### 3. [C] path, [A] meaning. Remote bullet copies receive owner messages the diagnostics never decode

**Location:** `game/lasertrace.cpp:134-137` (`Receive` snapshots before the original runs), `:92-115` (the snapshot
reads position, forward and endpoints, not the message), `:123` ("receive-before"); `docs/net-re/laser.md`
("Neither existing log establishes whether the symptom is delayed aim, pose, projectile construction or camera
orientation"). Disassembly for this review, `EDF.dll`:

- `LaserBullet01`'s NetworkBullet interface is the five-slot table at `0x179FD28` (RTTI `.?AVLaserBullet01@@`,
  offset `0x120`): slot 1 `0x22F3C0` owner, slot 2 `0x22F3F0` weak self, slot 3 `0x22F660` receive, slot 4
  `0x22F560` identity compare.
- Slot 3 reads one small int (`0x12B4BB0`, a thunk to the value reader `0x12B4660`) and dispatches: type 0 and
  type 3 (`0x233400`, `0x233590`) read two 12-byte bin elements (`0x12B49D0`) into the bullet at `+0xCD0` and
  `+0xCC0` (`+0x140` plus `0xB90`, `0xB80`); type 1 (`0x233690`) ORs 1 into `+0xE88`, reads a 12-byte bin, a
  value through `0x760C50`, and resolves an object reference with `0x784C60` (the same resolver hit authority uses
  for the attacker), then sets the byte at `+0xD40` and stores the reference at `+0xD48`; type 2 (`0x232E80`) ORs
  4 into `+0xE88`, reads an int, a 12-byte bin, a `0x760C50` value and a reference, and hands them to `0x2370D0`
  on the bullet's `+0x800`.
- `[A]`: the two vectors of types 0 and 3 are read as position and direction or velocity; type 1 as "attached to
  object X at offset P"; type 2 as "hit object X at P". The names are inferred from the reads, not from a sender
  trace.

So a copy is told where its owner's bullet is, what it hit and what it stuck to. Whether the copy then moves to
those values, and how far it was from them when they arrived, is the whole question of "their projectiles land
where they saw them land", and the plugin already sits on the receive with a hook that reads nothing of it.

**Failure scenario:** a player reports a teammate's laser hitting a wall on their screen while the teammate saw it
hit an ant. The logs on both machines hold snapshots of different shots (the eight-per-window cap picks
independently), the receive line shows the copy's state before the message, and nothing can be said.

**Fix shape:** in the existing `Receive` hook, after `originalReceive`, decode the message non-destructively with
the mark/rewind pair hit authority already uses (`PeekHitEventMessage`, `game/hitauthgame.cpp`): type, the two
vectors for types 0 and 3, the reference id for 1 and 2, bounded by `ReadBinElement`. Log them with the copy's
post-receive `+0x90` and `+0x80`, the owner id and a per-owner receive sequence, and on the owner's machine log the
same message as it is sent (the send side is the matching NetworkObject send, `0x773DA0` or the bullet's own; find
it from `0x233400`'s writer counterpart before hooking). Then one two-machine session, same shot matched by owner
and sequence, answers three questions at once: does the copy start where the owner's bullet started, does it
receive corrections, and how large is the error when a correction lands. The diagnostic stays bounded (eight per
window) and changes no behaviour. Only after that does a fix (advancing the copy by the one-way delay, or writing
the vectors through) have a target.

## Minor

### Player sync

**4. [C] `UpdateHook` takes and releases the lock for nothing.** `game/netplayer_game.cpp:261-262`. The pair at
the top of the hook guards no read and orders nothing. **Fix shape:** delete the two lines.

**5. [A] The four-entry track tables thrash at mission end and inherit a reused address.**
`game/netplayer_tracks.h:31` (`kPlayers = 4`), `game/netplayer_game.cpp:221`, `:244`, `:265` (`Find` with eviction
of the oldest). For about 12 s at the end of each mission the game holds two sets of players

address inherits the old track until `staleMs` (400 ms) passes. The assumption is that the doubled set also
runs `NetworkUpdate` and receives records. **Fix shape:** eight entries, and clear both tables when the lobby
view changes (the gate's observer already fires on it).

**6. [C] `[Diagnostics] Laser=1` hooks three vtable slots of every laser bullet in a stable release.**
`game/plugin.cpp:191`, `game/lasertrace.cpp`. The cost is small and bounded, but a diagnostic that has not found its
capture yet (finding 3) is on for every player by default. **Fix shape:** keep it on until finding 3's capture is
done, then default to off; or make the decoded version the one that is on, since it is the one that can answer.

### Hit authority

**7. [C] A comment gives a reason that no longer holds.** `game/hitauthgame.cpp:209` says the hooks never ask the

### Transport

**8. [C] A packet below the cumulative point after a Forward counts as a duplicate.** `src/reliable.cpp:226`. After
`onForward` moves `expected_` past given-up sequence numbers, a late copy of one of them is dropped, which is right,
and counted in `duplicates_`, which is not: it was never delivered. On a lossy link the `dup` figure in the STATS
line overstates spurious resends. **Fix shape:** a separate `late_` counter, or do not count below the floor
set by a Forward.

**9. [C] Two INI ranges clamp silently.** `src/config.cpp:354` (`LinkTimeoutMs`: read as 0 to 999999999, then
clamped to 3000 to 300000), `:370` (`GraceSeconds`: read as 0 to 999999999, then clamped to 1 to 600). `readInt`
warns only outside the read range, so `LinkTimeoutMs=1000` becomes 3000 and `GraceSeconds=0` becomes 1 without a
`CONFIG` line, while the README states the real ranges. **Fix shape:** pass the real range to `readInt` and drop the
clamp.

**10. [C] Every game send takes two locks to look for stranded data.** `src/eos_hooks.cpp:1046`
(`sendStranded`: `g.strandedMutex`, then `DirectNet::takeStranded`'s `mu_`, which the link thread holds through
`tick()` and `processDatagram`). Hundreds of game sends a second each wait for the link thread's slice. No
correctness issue; a `std::atomic<bool> hasStranded` set by `keepStranded` and `retire` skips both locks on the
common path. **Fix shape:** that flag.

### Spectator camera

**11. [C] The stale-aim line names the wrong cause for the most likely case.** `game/spectate.cpp:541-544` says

**12. [A] `Key::Pressed` reads the keyboard through `GetAsyncKeyState` while the game window is in the

## Cleared

- **Reliable upgrade and queues.** `hookSendPacket` upgrades `UnreliableUnordered` to `ReliableUnordered` for
  `ApiVersion >= 3` only, held peers get `bAllowDelayedDelivery`, queues only grow and 0 stays unlimited
  (`src/eos_hooks.cpp`, `configureHandle`, `hookSendPacket`).
- **Hold and lobby hold.** Transient reasons match the SDK enum; a deliberate close supersedes held events and
  reaches the game once; lobby statuses deliver members first, then the owner, then the room; the owner pin hides
  a promotion only while the pinned owner's link answers; callbacks run unlocked (`src/hold.cpp`).
- **Direct link health.** `routable` follows Pong/Ack within `kAnswerMs` plus RTT, recovers after `kRecoverMs`, a
  link that stops answering strands its pending data to EOS and the host returns relayed data to its sender; a
  joiner routes nothing until the host has answered with the link keys (`src/direct_net.cpp`, `updateHealth`,
  `linkUp`, `strand`, `returnToSender`). The earlier review's findings 1 and 2 are closed.
- **Reliable layer.** Karn's rule, RTO floor 150 ms with a quarter-SRTT margin, per-packet backoff, credit per ACK
  with a probe rate and a shared byte budget, Forward floors, 4096 reorder cap, 8 MB pending cap, `skipped`
  arithmetic (`src/reliable.cpp`).
- **Wire decode and fragments.** Header, protocol, tag and body bounds; strings and rosters capped; payload at
  most 1170; ack ranges capped and non-empty; fragment count and per-index length exact; 16 partials per sender,
  1 MB in all, 15 s progress timeout, completed ids remembered for 60 s; cleared on leaving the room
  (`src/wire.cpp`, `src/fragment.cpp`, `src/eos_hooks.cpp` `leftLobby`).
- **Link crypto.** Stateless cookies bound to address, id, nonce and time bucket; hellos signed by the identity the
  room published; welcomes signed over both ECDH keys and the roster; per-direction HMAC keys; counters taken
  once through a 1024 window; constant-time compares (`src/auth.cpp`, `src/wire.cpp`, `src/direct_net.cpp`).
- **Netcode gate.** A non-host ANDs the host's room caps; an unpublished member turns everything off; the host
  refuses a mismatched member only after it has shown it read the host's protocol; a feature that failed to
  install is withdrawn before publication (`game/netfeature.cpp`, `game/plugin.cpp`).
- **Hit authority.** Each hit is decided on exactly one machine across the four sender/owner rule combinations;
  enemies keep the original GDI; the 5 s settle and grace bracket the lobby skew; events are checked for finite
  numbers, owner, liveness, offset, range, a 64-wide sequence window with wrap, and a per-(sender, attacker) token
  bucket; the replay's attacker reference is an owned weak copy; non-owner copies read and rewind
  (`game/hitauth.cpp`, `game/hitauthgame.cpp`).
- **Player sync arithmetic.** Sequence and clock baseline with creep, 350 ms extrapolation, warp above
  `max(8 m, speed * 0.25 s)`, per-axis "applied as far as it moved" over a four-step window, the 4 ms re-entry
  guard, NaN guard on the copy's position (`game/netplayer.cpp`). The hooks check twelve instruction sequences
  and twenty slots before writing and restore in reverse on failure (`game/netplayer_game.cpp`).
- **Spectator camera.** Every range hashed before the call is rewritten; the factory's weak reference is the only
  one released; the view switch goes through the game's `weak_from_this` and view switch; a camera whose target
  expired or left is never left current; no game pointer survives a frame; `HudVisible` and `DrawCaption` read what
  the game's own functions read under the same checks (`game/spectate.cpp`).
- **Patching.** Every site verified before any write, writes restored in reverse on failure, thunk pages with
  unwind data registered, mid-hook thunks preserve the `CpuContext` layout (`game/plugin.cpp`, `game/code.cpp`,
  `game/midhook.h`).
- **Config.** UTF-8, UTF-16 and ANSI files; comments after values; a value not understood keeps its default with a
  `CONFIG` line, except the two ranges in finding 9 (`src/config.cpp`).

## Architecture notes

- **Four streams decide where a remote player and their shots appear, at three cadences, on one clock nobody
  shares.** The game's position (half floats, every sixth sync frame), the game's aim (every sixth sync frame on
  foot, never in a vehicle), the plugin's block (position and velocity with a sender timestamp, delivered one in
  three) and the owner's per-bullet messages (finding 3). Only the block carries a time. A remote copy's shot
  leaves from where the copy is (block-driven, good) in the direction the copy faces (game aim, stale) at the time
  the fire record arrives (one-way delay late) and then gets corrections the plugin has not read. Finding 3's
  capture ranks these; finding 1 fixes the one the camera needs regardless.
- **Hit authority already makes the outcome follow the shooter.** For enemies and objects the shooter's machine
  deals the damage with its own GDI; for players and vehicles it sends an event. So a copy's projectile landing
  elsewhere is a visual disagreement, not a gameplay one, except for enemy-to-player hits, which the host decides on
  its delayed view of the player. Any projectile sync work is about what players see; the damage path should not
  be touched for it.
- **Enemy positions are the game's own.** The same half-float, every-sixth-frame replication with no velocity
  applies to every host-owned enemy a joiner sees. A shooter aiming at an ant on their screen and a watcher seeing
  the laser miss it by a body length may be looking at two different ants. Extrapolating enemies the way players
  are extrapolated is the larger project behind "land in the same place"; it needs the same cadence answer as
  finding 2 first.
- **The locked camera and the remote copy's pose have one input.** Smoothing aim for the camera alone (finding
  1, first part) leaves the copy's pose stepping. Writing smoothed aim into the copy (second part) fixes both, with
  a wire change. The order is: camera first (no wire, one scope decision), then the capture, then the block.
- **Diagnostics are the research tool and should be built to answer one question each.** The motion trace
  answered the estimator windup; the laser trace was built before the question was known and so answers none.
  Finding 3's decode is the pattern: one hook, the exact fields, matched across machines by an id.
